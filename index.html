Executive summary · read this first

Two of these are buildable now. Four are waiting on something else.

Switzerland has strong open data on housing, elderly care, transit, energy and heat — almost none of it has been turned into a citizen- or planner-facing tool. Of the six systems assessed below, two are the fastest realistic starting points — one needs a data-access request more than a new law, the other needs a genuine product answer to proactive nudging rather than new data. The other four are blocked on dataset maturity, a health-sector partnership, or labor-market complexity outside what a small team controls.

01

Cross-Cantonal Permit Bottleneck Tracker

Housing — digitalization is happening canton-by-canton in isolation, and nobody tracks where approved units actually stall.

The gap

Zurich's eBaugesucheZH (live since April 2024) has no national equivalent. The federal government's 30-measure housing action plan has no public execution-tracking layer. Delays come from objections, appeals and municipal friction — not primarily construction capacity — but no dataset shows where applications stall.

What it'd do

Aggregate cantonal permit-portal data with GWR building starts, STATPOP growth and the Empty Dwellings Census into a live "pipeline slippage" map, flagging municipalities where approved-but-unbuilt units are piling up.

Why it's unbuilt

A data-integration problem, not a modeling one — non-uniform cantonal systems, most less mature than Zurich's.

Biggest challenge: Zurich's own open Baugesuche dataset deliberately strips names and street addresses — matching has to run on cadastral parcel number against GWR instead, a harder join since one parcel can hold several GWR entries. Records also drop out of public view after about a year, and the freely-open GWR tier updates only annually; near-real-time (48h) data needs a demonstrated-purpose authorization request to BFS or the canton, not just an API key.

Regulatory constraint: Low
Datasets combined
GWR — Register of BuildingsSTATPOPEmpty Dwellings CensusCantonal permit portals
Deep dive · data pipeline & MVP

1. Ingest

Daily pull of Zurich's open Baugesuche dataset (no street addresses — stripped by design) plus GWR building-register data. The free, no-registration GWR tier refreshes annually; near-real-time deltas require an authorized-access request to BFS or the canton, run as a parallel track alongside build. Annual STATPOP / Empty Dwellings Census releases round out the picture.

2. Match by parcel

Resolve each permit notice's cadastral parcel number — not street address, since Zurich's open dataset omits those — against GWR building IDs. One parcel can span several GWR entries, so this parcel-to-building join is the genuinely hard step, not the modelling.

3. Compute stall index

For each matched application, compare elapsed time since filing against that canton's median approval-to-groundbreak time. Flag anything past the 90th percentile with no matching GWR construction-start record.

4. Store

Postgres + PostGIS: three tables — permit_notice, building (from GWR), municipality_rollup — indexed by canton and municipality for the map layer.

5. Serve

Read-only REST endpoint + a vector-tile municipality choropleth by stall severity, refreshed nightly.

6. Publish

Public dashboard, plus the same API left open for cantonal housing offices (BWO) or journalists to query directly.

02

Cross-Cantonal Nursing Capacity Matching

Elderly care — shortage severity varies enormously by canton, but nothing routes staff to where the strain is worst.

The gap

Obsan's monitoring shows Central Switzerland alone needs 14,000 more nurses by 2029 — but each canton plans in isolation, with no shared view of relative shortfall.

What it'd do

A live capacity-vs-demand board combining Obsan workforce data, STATPOP age-cohort projections and hospital statistics, paired with an opt-in layer where agencies see cross-border shortfall hotspots.

Why it's unbuilt

Obsan's portal (July 2024) is monitoring-only — nobody has built the allocation layer on top of it.

Regulatory constraint: High

03

Distribution-Grid Stress Forecast & Flexibility Signal

Energy — a new 2M-point distribution grid dataset exists, unused by any citizen-facing tool.

The gap

Swiss-PDGs (published 2025) maps 2M+ low-voltage connection points. Swissgrid manages transmission-level congestion; local low-voltage stress from rooftop solar is unmanaged from a citizen standpoint.

What it'd do

Combine the distribution-grid dataset with Energy Dashboard PV data and MeteoSwiss irradiance to predict neighbourhood-level stress hours, then push a simple opt-in shift signal to households.

Why it's unbuilt

The underlying grid dataset is only months old — nobody has built on it yet.

Regulatory constraint: High for raw meter data

04

Heat-Vulnerability Early Warning

Climate — hotspot maps exist for planners; nothing links them to at-risk residents.

The gap

LCZ maps and the UMEP modelling tool are expert/planner-facing (QGIS). The Swiss Cities Association is currently calling for a national heatwave plan precisely because no coordination layer exists yet.

What it'd do

Overlay LCZ/UMEP hotspots with STATPOP elderly-density and GWR building-age to generate a ranked outreach list for municipal health services before heatwaves.

Why it's unbuilt

The modelling piece is done; the population-targeting and outreach-logistics layer is not.

Regulatory constraint: Low

05

Off-Peak Transit Advisor

Transport — real crowding-forecast data is already open; nothing acts on it.

The gap

SBB publishes real crowding forecasts (Occupancy Forecast, open file-based data, no registration needed) and already shows them reactively in timetable search. TransitFlow only visualises live positions. What's missing is the proactive layer — nobody nudges commuters toward a specific quieter departure before they go looking.

What it'd do

A rewards-based advisor — partnering with employers or season-ticket holders — that nudges commuters toward less-crowded departures using live forecast data.

Why it's unbuilt

A product gap, not a data gap — the data's been open for a while.

Biggest challenge: SBB already shows these occupancy forecasts directly in its own timetable search and app today, so the real gap isn't visibility — it's proactive personalized nudging and habit formation, a harder behavior-change problem than a data-integration one. It's also the more exposed position: SBB owns the data, the surface and the user relationship, and could ship native push nudges at any time.

Regulatory constraint: Low
Datasets combined
Occupancy Forecast (JSON)GTFS / GTFS-RTdata.sbb.ch flows
Deep dive · data pipeline & MVP

1. Ingest

Download the daily Occupancy Forecast archive (~100MB per operator, published once a day, covering the next 92 days) and parse to the defined commuter-corridor set; pull static GTFS nightly for schedule and stop structure. Coverage is SBB/BLS/Thurbo/SOB/Zentralbahn rail only — no trams or buses.

2. Normalize

Join each occupancy-forecast trip ID to its GTFS trip and stop sequence, so every scheduled departure carries a predicted crowding band — low, medium, high.

3. Personalize

User sets a home stop, work stop and an acceptable departure window (e.g. 07:15–09:00). The engine ranks that window's departures by predicted crowding.

4. Nudge

One ranked suggestion each morning via web push — "the 08:12 is forecast quieter than your usual 07:52" — and log which departure the user actually took.

5. Calibrate

Nightly job compares yesterday's forecasts against data.sbb.ch actuals to compute a rolling accuracy score, shown in-app as a trust indicator.

6. Serve

Installable PWA on mobile web — no app-store review cycle blocking iteration on the ranking model.

06

Municipal Coordination Layer

Cross-cutting — housing, care, transit, grid and heat are planned by separate offices with no shared view.

The gap

A housing development can be approved without visibility into whether the local grid, transit line, or nearby care facilities can absorb it.

What it'd do

A single-pane tool for municipal planners overlaying all five data layers so decisions account for compounding local strain rather than isolated sign-off.

Why it's unbuilt

The most ambitious idea here — it needs 01–05 to exist first as data layers, which is likely why nobody's attempted it.

Regulatory constraint: Depends on inputs
