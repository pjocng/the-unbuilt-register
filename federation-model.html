CH ← THE UNBUILT REGISTER / PLATFORM BRIEF

← The Unbuilt Register · companion brief #07 · grounded in production Swiss e-government infrastructure

Six systems can share almost everything about how they run. They can't share the data itself.

A federated platform — one identity layer, one interoperability registry, one secure exchange bus, one aggregate reporting layer, sitting under six independently-owned modules — is realistic. A single merged database is not: Swiss federalism splits ownership across cantons and the Confederation, and the revised Data Protection Act's purpose-limitation rule blocks quietly repurposing data collected for one job onto another.

Verdict

Federate the plumbing. Don't merge the data.

Realistic as one platform

Identity & login, an interoperability/schema registry, a secure exchange bus, shared reference registers, monitoring, and an aggregate-only reporting layer. Every one of these already runs in production at national scale — I14Y, sedex and eIAM/CH-LOGIN — and is exactly the shared infrastructure the "Digitale Verwaltung Schweiz" 2024–2027 strategy is pushing every agency toward anyway.

Not realistic as one platform

A single database or single agency owning all six domains' case-level data. No legal entity has that authority — housing permits, elderly care and heat outreach are cantonal/municipal matters, energy grids answer to distribution operators and ElCom, transit answers to the federal government and SBB. Art. 6 nDSG separately bars processing personal data for a purpose incompatible with why it was collected.

The shared substrate, one service at a time

None of this is hypothetical infrastructure. Each service below is modeled directly on a system already running Swiss government data exchange today.

Identity & Access

modeled on eIAM / CH-LOGIN

One login for citizens across all six front ends; role-scoped access for agency staff. The federal eIAM service already does exactly this for federal e-government apps, with "bring your own identity" support for cantonal and external providers.

Interoperability Registry

modeled on I14Y

A shared catalog of data, APIs and semantics so cantonal permit schemas, GWR fields and SBB occupancy formats mean the same thing platform-wide. Isolated modules publish their schema here too — never the data itself.

Secure Exchange Bus

modeled on sedex

Encrypted, audited, non-repudiated file transport already used by 8,000+ Swiss public-sector units across 80 domains. Carries aggregated extracts to the warehouse and specific encrypted handoffs — never a shared data lake.

Reference Registers

GWR · STATPOP · cadastral survey

Already-public, already-shared base data — buildings, population cohorts, parcels, municipality boundaries. Low sensitivity, reused directly by any module that needs geographic or demographic grounding.

Monitoring & Audit

operational, not data-sensitive

Uptime, incident response, SLA dashboards and access logs. Common infrastructure regardless of a module's sensitivity tier — it's metadata about the system, not the personal data flowing through it.

A realistic build order

The register already flagged 01 and 05 as the two systems buildable now — they're also the two lowest-sensitivity, most federatable modules. That makes them the natural pilot for the shared substrate, not a reason to build the whole platform at once.

STEP 1

Stand up identity, interoperability registry and exchange bus alongside 01 and 05 only. Prove the pattern on the two modules with nothing to isolate.

STEP 2

Wire the aggregate analytics warehouse to 01 and 05's outputs. Build Module 06's dashboard against real aggregate data for the first time.

STEP 3

Extend the substrate to 04, adding the point-to-point encrypted handoff pattern for its named list before any aggregate reporting is enabled.

STEP 4

Bring 02 and 03 in last, each on its own governance track — cantonal health law for 02, critical-infrastructure security review for 03.

Bottom line: yes, this can realistically become one platform — but "one platform" means one shared substrate, not one database. Built any other way, it either stalls on the legal review for 02 and 03, or ships something that quietly violates purpose limitation the day someone joins two tables that were never meant to be joined.
